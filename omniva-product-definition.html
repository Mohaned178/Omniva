Omniva Product Definition

The product definition for Omniva, a Personal Life Management Platform. It is built in three areas, each on top of the last: Product Scope, then User Journeys & Use Cases, then MVP Definition. Approved decisions and pending proposals are labelled separately, and nothing pending should be read as final.

Identity, vision and value
ItemDecision
CategoryPersonal Life Management Platform. Public description: "A connected platform for managing your personal life".
TaglineYour Life, All in One Place. Supporting line: Your Life, Connected.
StructureStructured core, flexible edges (custom fields, tags, custom item types).
ApproachHybrid: Omniva is the primary environment, and it integrates with specialists where appropriate.
VisionA world where people feel informed, prepared, and in control of the important aspects of their lives, with the clarity and confidence to make better decisions and focus on what truly matters.
MissionOmniva brings together and connects the different aspects of personal life, helping people organize their information, understand their circumstances, manage their responsibilities, and stay ahead of what matters through one private and intelligent platform.
Value headlineYour whole life, connected, so you always know where things stand and what's coming next.
Value pillarsSee · Connect · Anticipate · Control
IntelligenceOrganizer growing into a partner. Useful without AI. AI assists, the user decides.
Audience
  • Broad audience: individuals. Primary profile: people with complex lives (many areas to manage). Early adopters: self-organizers.
  • Entry-segment shortlist (to validate): self-organizers, people in major life transitions (expats as a sub-group), young couples building a shared life.
  • Participants: shared workspace members, and managed profiles (long-term). These are two separate concepts.
  • Geography: global product, regional entry. Arabic and English, with RTL/LTR designed in from the start.
Principles and conflict order
  • Your life, your control: private by default, user owns data, explicit and revocable sharing, no data monetization.
  • Honest and reliable: transparency, reliable above all.
  • Connected by design: never isolated, structured core with flexible edges, integrate rather than replicate.
  • Effortless and calm: low effort first, show only what's relevant, proactive but calm.
  • Grow with quality: useful without AI, depth before breadth, global and inclusive.
  • When principles conflict: trust & control → reliability → low effort & calm → connection → breadth.
Cross-cutting boundaries and long-term stages
  • Never moves money. Professional advice is informational only. Omniva prepares first, and delegated actions come later with approval. Users communicate through comments on items only.
  • Moving a capability out of "Out of scope" requires a deliberate, documented decision.
  • Stages: 1 Trusted Foundation → 2 Expanded Life (public launch) → 3 Proactive Life → 4 Life Partner. Progression is criteria-based.
  • Ecosystem: no public API or plugin marketplace. Export and calendar feeds come early, and user-authorized personal connections come in stage 3.

S1 · Platform structure Approved

How Omniva is organized as a product: what a module is, what is shared across modules, and where the cross-life experiences sit. This is a product structure, not a system architecture.

Approved · 24 Nov 2026
  • Structure: Option C, layered hybrid, with four conceptual layers: Foundation, Core Building Blocks, Life Modules (12), Cross-Life Experiences. It must support structured core, flexible edges, and connected by design.
  • Items and modules: every item has one canonical home module. Other modules may display or reference it when relevant. One source of truth, never duplicated records. Example: a gym membership lives in Subscriptions & Services, appears in Health & Fitness, and its cost connects to Finance & Wealth.
  • Custom item types: created inside the module where they belong, with a general fallback area for types that truly fit no module. Custom types always use the shared building blocks, so they stay connected.
  • Foundation scope: privacy, identity, permissions and sharing belong to the Foundation. The E2EE Vault is not an approved capability. It stays undecided.
  • Resolved later in S3b: Personal Analytics became embedded Insights. Automation became a behind-the-scenes shared capability. The E2EE Vault was approved in long-term scope as optional and limited.

Capabilities that work across all modules. Split into two parts: S3a core experiences (approved) and S3b advanced capabilities (approved).

Approved · S3a · 24 Nov 2026

Approved · S3b · 24 Nov 2026
CapabilityApproved definition
Activity Timeline & Security logTwo separate concepts. Activity Timeline: changes and actions on items (including automatic changes and future delegated actions), within permissions. Security log: sign-ins, devices, exports and sharing changes, visible to the account owner only. Rule: record changes, never views. No "last seen", no view tracking, and nothing about a member's private items. Life history is covered by past calendar entries and key dates.
AutomationA behind-the-scenes shared capability, not a user-facing experience. Committed core: built-in smart behaviours (renewals roll dates forward, expiry changes status, templates create default reminders, recurring obligations create expected costs). Every automatic change is visible in the Activity Timeline and can be undone. User-configurable rules are a possible future capability (S8).
Insights (Personal Analytics)Embedded in context: cost of ownership on an item, subscription totals, spending trends, plus short summaries in the Overview and digest. No separate analytics area. Informational only. Final user-facing wording is set during the journeys.
AI AssistantEmbedded first, with an optional "Ask" interface whose answers cite the records used. Can be switched off entirely at account level, and Omniva stays fully functional. All Product Vision 7.4 guardrails apply.
E2EE VaultIn long-term scope, limited: an optional protected area for sensitive files and notes only. Per item, the user chooses whether the title and key dates stay outside encryption. Mandatory recovery key. Plain-language explanation of its limits. Vault contents are excluded from server search, insights, AI and automation. Timing is an MVP / stage question.

S5 · Cross-module relationships Approved

How things connect across modules. Links are what turn twelve modules into one connected life.

Approved · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted
  • A defined set of named relationships with plain-language names, plus a generic "related to" for anything else.
  • Links are two-way: a link is visible from both sides (the car shows its insurance, and the insurance shows its car).
  • Links stay within one workspace. Connecting a personal item to a shared one means moving it into the shared workspace. When an item moves, the user chooses which related items move with it, and is shown what will become visible.
  • Plans group things across modules through "part of", without changing each item's home module.
  • Deleting or archiving never silently deletes linked items. The user sees what is linked and decides.

Rules for every integration

  • Opt-in, read-only by default, and revocable at any time.
  • The user sees exactly what is imported, and imported data follows the same ownership, export and deletion rules.

S8 · Long-term product vs future capabilities Approved

What is committed long-term scope, what is only a possible future capability, and what is not pursued.

Approved · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

Committed long-term scope

  • All 12 modules as defined in S4
  • The ten building blocks and the shared anatomy
  • All S3 capabilities, including the limited Vault
  • Shared workspaces and managed profiles
  • Legacy and emergency access (stages 3–4)
  • User-authorized personal connections (stage 3)
  • Delegated actions with every Area 6 safeguard (stage 4)
  • Text inside documents in search

Possible future capabilities

Evaluated later through the boundary-change rule. Not committed.

  • User-configurable automation rules
  • Two-way calendar sync
  • Private references across workspaces
  • Curated partner integrations (stage 4)
  • Offline editing on mobile
  • Voice assistants and smart devices
  • Distribution through organizations (no data access)

Not pursued

  • Public developer platform and plugin marketplace
  • General messaging or chat
  • Payments or moving money
  • A separate analytics area
  • Gamification (streaks, badges)
  • A separate desktop app
  • Everything marked Out of scope in the module boundaries

Managed profiles: approved concept (details later)

  • A managed profile is a Person with managed status. Their documents, health records and key dates belong to the managing user's workspace, personal or shared (for example, both parents).
  • The dependant has no access while managed.
  • When a dependant becomes an adult, the managing user can hand the profile over as the dependant's own account, transferring their records, with the dependant's consent.
  • Stage 3. Detailed permissions, privacy rules and edge cases are defined during specification.

J1 · Journey framework & personas Approved

How journeys are grounded (personas), written (format) and selected (criteria).

Approved · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J1.1 Personas: thin, situation-based, labelled as hypotheses. Each persona is defined by life load, current tools, trigger moment, fear, and what success means, and maps to an entry segment. They are replaced by interview evidence as validation happens and never justify a feature on their own. Rejected: demographic personas (contradict the life-complexity profile, invite invented detail); jobs-only with no personas (loses who does the work).

PersonaSituationEntry segment
P1 The self-organizerAlready runs life on spreadsheets, Notion and folders. Pain: upkeep, no reminders, nothing connected.Self-organizers (early adopters)
P2 The person in transitionA move, new job or new country causes a spike in documents and deadlines. Plans carry the load. Expats are a variant, not a separate persona.Major life transitions
P3 The couple building a shared lifeA private life and a shared life at once. Includes a secondary role: the less-engaged partner.Young couples
P4 The person carrying others (long-term)Manages records for children or an aging parent. Keeps journeys from ruling out managed profiles. Stage 3.None yet (long-term)

Check only, not personas: the "not designed for" profiles (simple to-do seeker, specialist power user). A journey that mainly serves them is a warning sign.

J1.2 Journey format: short narrative plus a structured breakdown: trigger · goal · status quo without Omniva · steps (what the user does / what Omniva does) · connections that make the difference · where value appears on the value timeline · effort points and failure risks · capabilities used (S references) · gaps found (to J6). No screens or UI flows. Rejected: user-story lists (become feature lists); detailed UX storyboards (too early).

J1.3 Qualifying criteria: a journey is included only if (a) it involves at least two modules or blocks in a way that matters to the user, (b) the status quo fails at a specific, nameable moment, and (c) it fits a persona's real trigger. Each journey is tagged episodic / recurring / daily. No requirement to cover every module: a module absent from strong journeys is an MVP finding, not something to patch.

J2 · Foundational journeys Approved

The journeys every user goes through regardless of persona. Split into Part 1 (first session, first weeks) and Part 2 (capture, finding things, digest and weekly review).

Approved · Part 1 · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J2.1 First session: trigger-led life inventory (P1, P2 · episodic · value timeline: first session). Optional "What brought you here?" (moving, new car, getting organized, renewals), then 5–8 tailored prompts, each asking only what the thing is and the one key date that matters. Omniva shows the default reminder it will set (visible, editable), offers connections in natural language ("Is this insurance for your car?"), and lands on an Overview with real upcoming dates and cards only for areas used. Plain-language privacy promise up front. Document upload is optional in the first session (can go to the Inbox later). Import is a side path for self-organizers. Goal: one working connection visible in session one. Rejected: module picker with an empty Overview (abandonment); import-first as default (effort; extraction is opt-in); a plain inventory without a trigger.

J2.2 First weeks: the first reminder that matters (all · episodic turning recurring · value timeline: first weeks). The return path is the calm digest, not engagement tricks. The first reminder that prevents a miss is the moment of truth, so reliability dominates. Digest suggestions are connection-driven and capped: at most 1–2 per digest, always based on something already in Omniva ("You added a car. Add its insurance renewal?"), never generic, and can be switched off. Rejected: no suggestions (connected value never appears); progress bars or completeness scores (gamification).

Gap sent to J6: missing key date ("I don't know the date"). Suggestion to evaluate: allow it, mark "date missing", surface gently in the attention list. Note for M8: "first useful reminder within 10 minutes" fits J2.1.

Approved · Part 2 · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J2.3 Capture: optional quick hints (P1, P2 · recurring, irregular). Stage-1 capture works in a mobile browser and through email forwarding. At capture, one optional "link to…" a recent or likely item and an optional "is there a date?"; skipping sends it to the Inbox. Simple rule-based suggestions (a forwarded email from a known sender matches an existing subscription). AI extraction stays opt-in, later. Rejected: classify at capture (friction); Inbox-only (graveyard, no connections).

J2.4 Finding things: search plus the connected item page as the hub (all · recurring, often urgent). People search by the thing, not the document type, so results show the item with what is attached to it. The item page shows everything about it (invoice, warranty, insurance, maintenance, costs). Saved smart views for recurring needs. Natural-language questions stay with the optional Ask. Risk noted: urgent lookups on the go; stage 1 is online mobile web, offline read comes with native mobile (stage 2). Rejected: search-only flat list; module browsing only.

J2.5 Digest and weekly rhythm (all · recurring). Weekly by default, adjustable (weekly / every two weeks / monthly); urgent items notify directly. Contents: coming up, decisions before renewals (keep or cancel, trials ending), Inbox to sort, 1–2 connection suggestions, one short insight; every entry actionable (done, snooze, open). A quiet week sends a one-line "nothing needs you this week" (can be turned off). No separate weekly-review feature: the digest plus the Overview is the review. Rejected: daily digest (noise); only-when-needed (silence reads as broken).

Gap sent to J6: rule-based (non-AI) matching at capture is not explicitly defined in scope.

J3 · Cross-module signature journeys Approved

The journeys where connection makes the clearest difference. Each is detailed one at a time.

Approved · J3.0 and J3.1 · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J3.0 The set: six signature journeys, detailed in this order: car → something breaks → renewal season → trip → move → new baby (new baby marked long-term: needs managed profiles, stage 3). Approved · J3.2 · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J3.2 Something breaks (P1 · episodic · value timeline: months onward). The washing machine stops 20 months after purchase. The user searches for it; the item page shows the warranty status ("Under warranty until Mar 2027") with the invoice attached, possible other cover, past repairs and the technician who did them. Omniva shows the provider's contact details and the documents needed; it prepares, never files the claim. The claim is tracked as a task; the outcome is a repair Event (plus a cost if paid) or a new item that replaces the old one.

Honest limitation: the journey only pays off if the invoice and warranty were captured earlier. If not, it becomes "add it now". This is the strongest argument for low-effort capture and likely the strongest demo story.

J3.2a Claims: tracked as a task with notes (reference number) and a follow-up date; reimbursements as Money records. A dedicated Claim type is not added now.

Sent to J6: a Claim template candidate (insurance and health claims may recur); indirect coverage ("possibly covered by", following policy covers property → item located at property; rule-based, informational, "check your policy"); item-level document download (a claim pack).

Approved · J3.3 · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

J3.3 Renewal season: "what am I paying for?" (P1, P3 · recurring · value timeline: first session and months onward). A checklist of common categories and named services ("Which of these do you pay for?") needs only price and cycle. The All recurring commitments view shows subscriptions, insurance, utilities, rent or mortgage, loans and memberships with monthly and yearly totals and a renewal timeline. Renewal decisions arrive in the digest. Cancelling: Omniva shows how, creates a task, and marks it ended only when the user confirms. Price changes are recorded and shown in history. Insights: totals by area, year-on-year change, and overlapping cover (two policies covering the same item, from direct covers links). Honest limitation: manual until bank import (stage 2–3, region-dependent).

J3.3a Reminder defaults by type: free trials (a few days before), annual commitments (about 30 days before), price changes. Monthly subscriptions get no reminder by default and appear in totals. All editable. Rejected: remind on every renewal (noise); no reminders (trials convert).

J3.3b "Ask, never assume" is a cross-cutting rule: Omniva never marks a real-world commitment as ended, cancelled or paid on its own. It asks and the user confirms. (Generalizes the S4 disposal clarification; see the J5 note on auto-renewing dates.)

J3.3c Service catalog: names and categories only, never prices (prices go stale and become wrong data). Contents follow the entry region. A templates detail within S3a capture.

Noted for M2: "What am I paying for?" is a strong first-session trigger (meaningful total within minutes).

M1 · Target users & primary problem Approved

Who the MVP is for and the problem it solves. An MVP assumption, not a change to Omniva's identity; validation (M7) can overturn it.

Approved · 24 Nov 2026 · Claude's recommendations accepted

M1.1 Entry-segment assumption: self-organizers, with life transitions as triggers within that group (a move, a new car, renewal season), not a separate segment. Young couples stay a stage-2 segment, so shared workspaces stay in stage 2 and the conditional move to stage 1 is not triggered. Risk recorded: self-organizers are the hardest group to win from the status quo, which makes them a demanding validation test. Rejected as the MVP assumption: life transitions alone (episodic, hard to reach at the right time); young couples (forces shared workspaces into stage 1, two-sided adoption).

M1.2 Primary problem: "I keep my life organized by hand, but the information is scattered and nothing tells me what's coming, so things slip and I can't find what I need when it matters." Connection is how Omniva solves it, not the pitch. The two proofs users can feel: nothing important slips (Anticipate) and anything can be found in seconds, with everything related to it (See + Connect). These feed M8.

M1.3 Region and language assumption: region-neutral, English first. Multi-currency on; no region-dependent features in the MVP; service catalog generic; Arabic and RTL designed in from the start and launched in stage 2 as approved. Revisit if a specific entry region (especially an Arabic-speaking one) is chosen.
